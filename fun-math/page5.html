River Crossing I: The Royal Rescue! 👑

The royal chess family is stranded! The King, the Queen and their loyal Pawns must cross the river — but the little raft has very strict rules. Can you find the smartest plan that breaks NO rules?

🛶 The story: A chess tournament starts on the far bank — and the whole royal family must get there using one small raft. It carries at most 2 pieces, and it can NEVER glide across by itself!

🧠 This is a famous logic puzzle that is over 1,000 years old. Your mission is not just to get everyone across — it's to do it in the fewest trips possible. That's called optimization: finding the BEST solution!

🎯 Goal: bring EVERY piece safely across — in as few raft trips as possible!
🛶

Raft Capacity

The raft holds a maximum of 2 pieces on any single trip.

🚣

No Ghost Raft!

The raft can't move alone — at least one piece who knows how to row must be aboard.

👑

The King's Safety

The King can't row, and must never be left alone — on a bank or on the raft — without a loyal follower!

♕

The Queen's Rule

The Queen CAN row… but only when she is completely alone on the raft. No passengers, ever!

♙

The Pawn's Rule

Pawns are the friendly rowers — they happily paddle alone or with any other piece.

🧠 The Math Behind the River

Why is this a MATH adventure? Because solving it uses real mathematical superpowers:

🔢

Counting trips

Level 1 takes 7 trips, Level 3 takes 11. Spot the pattern? Every extra Pawn adds exactly 2 trips — predicting patterns is powerful math!

📏

Lower bounds

We checked EVERY possible plan by computer (breadth-first search) and proved no plan can beat 7–9–11. Finding the true best is called optimization!

🔎

Systematic trying

There aren't that many plans! Listing them all is called enumeration — the seed of computer science.

↩️

Working backwards

"Who must row back at the end?" Thinking from the goal backwards cracks many puzzles faster.

🗺️

State spaces

Each arrangement of pieces is a "state"; solving means finding a path of states. That's how GPS and game AI think!

💪

Resilience

A rule-break isn't failure — it's information! "Oops, the King was alone" teaches you what to avoid next try.

👪 This puzzle belongs to the same ancient family as wolf–goat–cabbage and the missionaries and cannibals — logic puzzles people have loved for over 1,000 years! Computers solve them using breadth-first search: "try everything systematically" — exactly what smart puzzle-solvers do. Want a twist?
